#### Aim: 
To determine the viscosity of a liquid by measuring the terminal velocity of a falling sphere

## Materials Required
- Tall Transparent Tube/Measuring Cylinder (500mL)
- Steel Ball Bearings Of Varying Diameters
- Rubber Bands
- Micrometer Screw Gauge
- Stopwatch
- Digital Balance
- Viscous Liquid (Soap or Washing up liquid)
- Magnets

## Procedure
- Measure the diameter $(d)$ of the first ball bearing using a micrometer at several orientations to find an average. calculate the radius $(r)$
- Measure the mass $m$ of the ball bearing using the balance and calculate its density $(\rho)$
- Determine the density of the liquid $(\rho)$ by measuring the mass of a known volume of the liquid
- Mark reference points: wrap two rubber bands around the cylinder. The first should be low enough to ensure the ball has reached terminal velocity before passing it
- Drop the ball: Release the ball bearing into the center of the cylinder (to avoid wall effects)
- Time the fall: Start the stopwatch when the bottom passes the passes the first marker and stop it at the second marker
- Repeat: Perform three timings for each diameter to calculate a mean time. Repeat the process for at least three different diameters of ball bearings

## Analysis
- At terminal velocity, the forces are balanced:
- $a = \frac{v-u}{t}$
- $Weight = Drag + Upthrust$
 $$mg = 6\pi \eta rv + \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \rho_f g$$
- By substituting the $m = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\rho_s$, we can rearrange the equation into the form $y=mx$
$$v=\frac{2g[\rho_s - \rho_f]}{9\eta}r^2$$
- Plot the graph of $v$ against $r^2$
- The graph should be a straight line through the origin
- The gradient $(m)$ of the line is $\frac{2g[\rho_s - \rho_f]}{9\eta}$
- To find viscosity $(\eta) = \frac{2g[\rho_s - \rho_f]}{9m}$

## Critical Notes & Sources of Error
- Zero Error: Check the micrometer and balance for zero errors before starting.
- Terminal Velocity: If the first marker is too high, the ball may still be accelerating, Move it further down.
- Parallax Error: Ensure measurements of the ball passing the rubber bands are taken at eye level. 
- Wall Effect: If the ball is too close to the side of the cylinder, the flow of liquid is restricted, increasing the effective drag.
- Temperature: Viscosity is highly temperature-dependent. Ensure the liquid temperature remains constant throughout the experiment.

## Safety Precautions
- Spillage: Viscous liquids are incredibly slippery. Clean up drips immediately.
- Glassware: Handle the tall cylinder with care; it is top-heavy when filled.
- Magnets: Use a magnet to retrieve balls rather than sticking hands into chemicals.
