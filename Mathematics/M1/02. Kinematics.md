# 02. Straight-Line Kinematics (Uniform Acceleration)

Kinematics describes the motion of points or bodies without considering the underlying forces causing the motion. This section focuses on one-dimensional motion under **constant linear acceleration**.

## 1. Core Variables
* $s$: Displacement (net vector change in position from the start point, in meters $\text{m}$)
* $u$: Initial velocity (vector speed at $t = 0$, in $\text{m/s}$)
* $v$: Final velocity (vector speed at time $t$, in $\text{m/s}$)
* $a$: Constant acceleration (rate of change of velocity, in $\text{m/s}^2$)
* $t$: Time elapsed (scalar, in seconds $\text{s}$)

---

## 2. Derivation & The Five *suvat* Equations
The equations are derived assuming acceleration is constant ($a = \text{constant}$):

1. **From definition of acceleration:** $a = \frac{v - u}{t} \implies$
   $$v = u + at$$
2. **From average velocity for uniform acceleration:** $v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{u + v}{2}$. Since $s = v_{\text{avg}} \cdot t \implies$
   $$s = \frac{1}{2}(u + v)t$$
3. **Substituting (1) into (2):** $s = \frac{1}{2}(u + u + at)t \implies$
   $$s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$$
4. **Substituting $u = v - at$ into (2):**
   $$s = vt - \frac{1}{2}at^2$$
5. **Eliminating $t$ by substituting $t = \frac{v - u}{a}$ into (2):** $s = \frac{1}{2}(u+v)\left(\frac{v-u}{a}\right) = \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2a} \implies$
   $$v^2 = u^2 + 2as$$

> [!WARNING] Sign Convention Matrix
> You must strictly define a positive direction (e.g., upwards = positive) before solving any problem. If upward is positive, downward gravity becomes $a = -9.8\text{ m/s}^2$.

---

## 3. Graphical Kinematics
Graphs provide a visual layout of motion parameters over time.

### A. Displacement-Time ($s\text{-}t$) Graphs
* **Gradient:** The derivative $\frac{ds}{dt}$ represents the **velocity**.
  * Constant linear slope = Constant velocity.
  * Curved line = Accelerating/decelerating motion (changing gradient).
* **Y-Intercept:** Represents the initial position relative to the origin.

### B. Velocity-Time ($v\text{-}t$) Graphs
* **Gradient:** The derivative $\frac{dv}{dt}$ represents the **acceleration**.
  * Flat horizontal line ($m=0$) = Zero acceleration (constant speed).
  * Positive linear slope = Uniform acceleration.
  * Negative linear slope = Uniform deceleration.
* **Area Under the Curve:** The definite integral $\int v \, dt$ yields the **net displacement**.
  * *Important distinction:* Total **distance** is calculated by treating areas below the time axis as positive absolute values. **Displacement** treats areas below the axis as negative values.
